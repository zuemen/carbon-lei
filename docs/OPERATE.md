# Operating CarbonLEI: who runs what

This document says who runs which part of CarbonLEI in a deployment, and what each role sends and pays for.

Each row names code in this repository or a section of these docs. Gas per operation, and its price at mainnet and estimated L2 fees, is in [PILOT §6](PILOT.md#6-costs-test-network-and-production-chain); this page gives no money amounts.

## Operating model at a glance

Only the first column describes what exists. The pilot column restates the plan in [PILOT §2](PILOT.md#2-roles-and-what-each-must-provide); the production column lists options from [ADOPTION §3](ADOPTION.md#3-who-pays) and [§5.1](ADOPTION.md#51-participation-when-the-cap-binds). Before the network phase, the team hands the allowlist to a governance body with `transferOwnership` to that body's multisig, and the new owner removes the watchers it does not keep (incident procedure 4). ## Static, browser-only, no server

- The hosted page: a static build on GitHub Pages ([`.github/workflows/pages.yml`](../.github/workflows/pages.yml)) that runs the checks in the browser and reads Sepolia through public RPC nodes ([ADOPTION §8](ADOPTION.md#8-current-status-by-area)).
- The Communication Template importer in the Supplier tab, which parses the file in a Web Worker and uploads nothing ([README](../README.md#start-from-the-commissions-communication-template)).
- `carbonlei verify`, which never sends a transaction ([`sdk/cli.ts`](../sdk/cli.ts)).
- `carbonlei serve` is the one exception to "no server": an HTTP API the importer runs on its own machine, never operated by CarbonLEI ([REST API](#rest-api-carbonlei-serve-deployment-and-security)).

## Needs an operator

KERI agents of the body and its auditors, the watcher process and its key, the allowlist owner key, and a wallet for each party that sends a transaction ([PILOT §3](PILOT.md#3-what-runs-where)).

## Incident procedures

Steps that avoid the contract red-team findings on the deployed contracts, which are not redeployed for them ([SECURITY §4.2](SECURITY.md#42-findings-of-the-contract-red-team-review-7-october-2026)). The contract does not enforce any of them.

1. **A body's EVM key may have leaked: suspend first, then rotate (CR1, CR9).** The watcher sends `suspendVerifier` for the body at once: a suspended body cannot register or revoke, so the stolen key can no longer revoke the body's reports, and a revocation cannot be undone. Only then does the allowlist owner send `rotateVerifierAddress` to the body's new key, and the watcher sends `liftSuspension` once the new key is in place. Rotating first leaves the stolen key able to revoke until the rotation is mined. The verifier marks reports the stolen key registered within 24 hours before the suspension or rotation as CONTESTED, and a revocation sent from an address that is rotated away within 24 hours after it. A suspension alone does not contest a revocation (a legitimate revocation of a falsified report often comes with one), so the rotation is part of the response.
   **Detection limits.** The rules on the revoking address and on reports registered just before the response look at the time between the theft and the response, and a thief can wait: a body key found more than 24 hours after a revocation it sent leaves that revocation INVALID, and reports it registered more than 24 hours before the response VALID unless they changed the supplier address (the verifier contests any revision chain with another supplier address, whenever it happened). An address that a stolen owner key rotated in is judged by its tenure instead: if the owner rotates it away within 30 days of its rotation in (`minAddressTenureDays`), every report it registered or revoked is CONTESTED, whenever the thief acted. So the watcher alerts on every `VerifierAddressRotated` and `ReportRevoked` of the bodies it watches, and the owner answers an unexpected rotation within 30 days and an unexpected revocation within 24 hours. A larger `contestedWindowHours` or `minAddressTenureDays` gives more time, at the cost of more false positives from routine rotations (below). While the body is suspended, any other active body can take over its periods (T19 (7)), so the watcher tells the replacing bodies first. The body keeps its signing key for revocations in a separate wallet from its day-to-day registration key, where its tooling allows it.
2. **Before a body's accreditation expires: hand over (CR3).** An accreditation cannot be renewed on this deployment, and after `accreditedUntil` any active body can revise the body's credentials for good, with another supplier or more tonnes. Before that date the body, an agreed successor and the watcher hand over: the watcher suspends the body, and the successor at once revises each open credential, under its own verification report ID, with the same supplier address and the same `verifiedKg` (a takeover is first-come, T19 (7)). From then on the successor is the active issuer of those credentials and no third body can revise them. The verifier does not flag such a revision, nor the shipments claimed before it; it marks one by another body that moves the credential to another layer (CN code or route), raises the tonnes or changes the supplier address as CONTESTED. The suspension does flag the body's reports registered within the 24 hours before it (4l), so the body registers nothing in the day before the handover.
3. **Before a downward correction: freeze first (CR2).** A revision below the quantity already claimed reverts, and the supplier can claim the rest ahead of it. The body first registers a revision whose `verifiedKg` equals the quantity claimed so far (it cannot revert for over-claiming), then settles the difference with the supplier and its importers, or revokes. On a production chain it sends corrections through a private transaction channel.
4. **When the allowlist owner changes: remove leftover watchers (CR4).** `transferOwnership` does not remove WATCHER holders, and the contract cannot list them. The new owner lists every holder from the allowlist's `RoleGranted` and `RoleRevoked` events for `WATCHER_ROLE` and revokes each one it does not keep.

**V2 only (not deployed): run the V2 monitor.** The CR1 delays in `contracts/src/v2/` help only if someone reacts within 72 hours ([SECURITY §13.1](SECURITY.md#131-cr1-rotation-and-revocation-by-a-freshly-installed-address)). On a V2 deployment the watcher runs `WATCHER_PRIVATE_KEY=… npm run watch:v2 -- <config.json>` (start from `verifier/config/watch-v2.example.json`; `--dry-run` alerts without sending, `--once` polls once). By default it cancels any rotation proposal not listed in `expectedRotations`; suspends a body after a rotation that was neither listed nor signed by an old address that was mature (bound 72 hours) and not itself installed by an unexpected rotation, judging a chain of rotations as a whole (a hostile address consenting to the next one does not make it trusted); cancels held revocations not listed in `expectedRevocations`; cancels WATCHER_ROLE grant proposals for accounts not in `expectedWatchers`; re-suspends a body it suspended if anyone other than its own key or `trustedLifters` lifts the suspension; and alerts, without blocking, on new bodies and auditors not in `expectedBodies` / `expectedAuditors`, ownership transfers not to `expectedOwners`, role grants and revocations, suspensions by others, supersedes and takeovers. It reads events only up to the head minus `confirmations` (default 12) and re-checks every proposal it cancelled until that proposal's `readyAt`, so a cancel lost in a reorganisation is repeated; it halves the `getLogs` range when the RPC refuses one, and refuses to run if the registry reads another allowlist. It prints each alert as a JSON line and, with `V2_WATCH_WEBHOOK_URL` set, also POSTs it there. Before proposing a routine rotation, the owner gets the body's confirmation out of band and adds the pair to `expectedRotations` (or, if the body's current address has been bound for 72 hours, the body signs the consent and the rotation executes at once); the same goes for a new watcher (`expectedWatchers`, then `proposeRoleGrant`, executable after 72 hours). To lift a suspension the monitor made, use the watcher key or an address in `trustedLifters`; an expected rotation clears the monitor's taint on the body. Rescue for a stolen body key: the watcher suspends the body, the owner proposes the new address (listed in `expectedRotations`), and after 72 hours anyone executes; the stolen key cannot cancel while the body is suspended. The state file (`verifier/.data/watch-v2-state.json`) lets a restarted monitor catch up on what it missed, so a restart within 72 hours is enough; the monitor is tested on a local chain only, its rule for WATCHER grants is in the Maude model (since 14 October) while its rules for lifts, new bodies and supersedes are not, and a monitor and body both down for more than 72 hours, a stolen watcher key, or an owner key not behind a timelock that first revokes the monitor's WATCHER_ROLE (SECURITY §13.1, finding C1: the body alone cannot then stop the rotation) defeat it.

## Routine key rotation

A body's address is rotated on a schedule (yearly or quarterly) or when staff with access leave. Done this way it raises no CONTESTED result:

1. The new address is a fresh key, used for nothing before.
2. In the 24 hours before the rotation the body registers no report and revokes none with the old address: a report registered within 24 hours before its address is rotated away is CONTESTED (CR9), and so is a revocation (CR1), because that is what the response to a stolen key looks like.
3. The owner sends `rotateVerifierAddress`.
4. In the 24 hours after the rotation the body registers no report that its supplier claims at once: a shipment claimed within 24 hours after the registering address was rotated in is CONTESTED.
5. The new address stays in use for at least 30 days. An address rotated in and away again within 30 days is treated as one a stolen owner key installed: its reports and revocations are CONTESTED for good. A rotation done by mistake is therefore left in place for 30 days, or its reports are re-issued after review.

Shipments of reports the old address registered stay VALID across the rotation; the verifier no longer flags claims near a rotation or a suspension of the body. A suspension by the NAB flags only the reports registered within 24 hours before it (4l); a revocation it follows stays INVALID.

**False-positive estimate** (assumes registrations, claims and revocations spread evenly over the year, and nothing else unusual): without the procedure, each rotation or suspension makes about 24 h / 365 days ≈ 0.27 % of the body's yearly reports CONTESTED; the earlier rules (before 7 October 2026) also flagged every claim within 48 hours around the event, about 0.55 % of all the body's claims per event (2.2 % a year with quarterly rotation). With steps 2 and 4, none.

## If the operator stops

Here "operator" means the trust-registry operator, which holds the allowlist owner key and the watcher key, and whoever hosts the static page; in the prototype and the pilot that is the team. This section is derived from the contract source and has not been tested as a scenario.

**What keeps working**

- **The records stay readable and cannot be changed.** The contracts have no proxy, no upgrade function and no pause ([SECURITY §5](SECURITY.md#5-smart-contract-assurance), "Why the contracts are not upgradeable"). `EmissionsClaimRegistry` has no owner; it holds only an immutable reference to the allowlist. Registered reports, the tonnage ledger, claims and revocations stay on the chain for as long as the chain runs, and no key can edit them.
- **Anyone can still verify.** `carbonlei verify` (MIT licence) runs checks 1–5 against any node of the chain (`--rpc`) and checks 6–7 on the evidence carried in the proof. The hosted page is a static build that anyone can rebuild from this repository and host elsewhere. No server of the operator is involved.
- **Listed bodies keep registering, revoking and being claimed against.** `registerReport` and `revokeReport` need only an active body (until its `accreditedUntil`) and, for registration, an auditor authorised on the allowlist; `claimShipment` needs only the report's supplier. None of them calls the owner or the watcher.
- **An importer can still** verify each proof, read the remaining tonnage, cite its claim transaction, export the PACT JSON and print the importer's file.

**What stops**

- **Listing and address changes.** Only the owner can call `addVerifier`, `addAuditor` and `rotateVerifierAddress`. No new body or auditor can be listed, a body that loses its EVM key cannot move to a new one, and an auditor who leaves cannot be replaced. An accreditation cannot be renewed on this deployment in any case (incident procedure 2), so once every listed body's `accreditedUntil` has passed, the deployment accepts no new reports.
- **Incident response.** Without the watcher key nobody can suspend a body, so the first step of incident procedure 1 is impossible, and a body that is suspended stays suspended, because nobody can lift it.
- **Revocation sync.** If the watcher stops, ECR revocations and accreditation withdrawals are no longer mirrored on-chain. A verifier then sees a report registered under an auditor whose ECR was revoked off-chain pass on-chain and pass check 4. Check 7 reads the auditor's status from the allowlist at the registering block, so it passes as well, unless the presented evidence itself carries a signed, anchored revocation dated before the registration ([SECURITY T18](SECURITY.md#4-threat-model)). The CONTESTED window is measured from a sync, so with no sync nothing is flagged. The chain shows no sign that the watcher has stopped, and nothing alerts anyone.

**Handing over**

- **Planned.** The owner calls `transferOwnership` to a successor's key, which also hands over the administration of the watcher role, and the successor removes leftover watchers (incident procedure 4). This needs the old owner key: if the operator disappears without handing over, nobody can take over this allowlist.
- **Unplanned.** The only route is a new deployment ([SECURITY §5, redeployment procedure](SECURITY.md#5-smart-contract-assurance)): new addresses and a new EIP-712 domain, so every credential in use is signed again, and reports with claimed shipments stay on the old contracts, because moving them would start a second tonnage ledger. The contracts do not link reports across deployments (`supersedes` names a report in the same registry only). Without the old watcher key the old deployment cannot be closed to new registrations either, because that step suspends every body on the old allowlist; its bodies can keep registering there until their `accreditedUntil`.
- **The chain itself.** Records last only as long as the chain they are on. Sepolia is a test network; the same question applies to whichever production chain is chosen.

## Accreditation records: what the allowlist operator records

The on-chain allowlist entry holds hashes only (the body's LE and accreditation credential SAIDs, `accreditedUntil`). Next to it the trust-registry operator keeps one off-chain record per listed body, so that a buyer can see who accredited the body and check 8 can compare it with each report ([SECURITY §4.5](SECURITY.md#45-check-8-sub-rules-on-the-verification-report)). Format: [`fixtures/accreditation-registry.json`](../fixtures/accreditation-registry.json) (`carbonlei-accreditation-record/1`, read by `carbonlei verify --accreditation-registry <file>` and the hosted page). The fields follow the registration data a verifier gives the CBAM Registry under Art 10a(2) of the CBAM Registry Act, as summarised in §10.2 of the Commission's guidance of 24 August 2026:

| Field | What the operator records, and from where |
|---|---|
| `verifierLEI`, `verifierName`, `countryOfEstablishment` | The body's LEI (the same as in its LE vLEI), name and country of establishment |
| `accreditationNumber` | The unique accreditation identification on the NAB's certificate |
| `nab.name`, `nab.country`, `nab.lei`, `nab.registerUrl` | The national accreditation body and its Member State; the NAB's public register entry where the operator confirmed the accreditation |
| `standards` | EN ISO/IEC 17029:2019 with EN ISO 14065, as stated on the certificate |
| `activityGroups`, `cnScope` | The CBAM activity groups of Annex I to Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/2551 (for example VIII, iron or steel products) and the CN codes the accreditation credential lists |
| `validFrom`, `validUntil` | The certificate's effective and expiry dates (at most five years, Art 51 AVR-DA); `validUntil` equal to the on-chain `accreditedUntil` |
| `administrativeMeasures` | Each suspension, withdrawal or scope reduction with the date it took effect and, when lifted, the date it ended; the operator adds it the day it learns of it, as it sends `suspendVerifier` |
| `accreditationSaidHash` | `hashString` of the accreditation credential's SAID, the value in the allowlist entry |
| `certificateSha256` | The SHA-256 of the certificate copy the operator holds (optional) |
| `commissionList` | Whether the Commission's list of accredited CBAM verifiers is published, the date the operator last checked, and the page; until it is, check 8 shows a note with that date (unpublished on 16 October 2026) |

The record is the operator's statement, not the NAB's: check 8 turns any difference into a warning for review, never a pass. The operator re-checks each record against the NAB's register when it lists a body, at every change the NAB notifies, and before `validUntil`, and re-checks the Commission page until the list is published.

## Importer workbench: planning a year's declaration

An importer with many suppliers does not verify one proof at a time. The workbench verifies all of a year's proofs against one block, then plans the CBAM declaration from the verdicts: per line, the supplier's verified value or the default with the year's mark-up (and why), totals, the 50 t de minimis status, the quarterly holding, the certificates and their gross €, and a CSV worksheet. Code: [`sdk/portfolio.ts`](../sdk/portfolio.ts) (pure, no chain), [`sdk/batch.ts`](../sdk/batch.ts) and two CLI commands; the hosted page's **Importer workbench** tab runs the same `buildPortfolio` on a six-line illustrative portfolio. It is a planning aid: it does not submit anything, it is not a CBAM Registry format, and it sends no transaction ([REGISTRY_EXPORT](REGISTRY_EXPORT.md): the Commission has published no data structure for the definitive-period declaration).

**Procedure (one importer, one year)**

1. Collect each supplier's proof file and write a manifest with what customs knows about each consignment ([example](../fixtures/workbench/manifest.json)): `{ "eori": "<your EORI>", "shipments": [{ "proof": "proofs/a.json", "id": "<your line id>", "quantityTonnes": "200", "cnCode": "73181595", "country": "TW", "importDate": "2026-10-10" }, …] }`. A line with `"proof": null` is planned on the default. A folder of proof files also works; the quantity and date then come from each proof's shipment, and only when check 5 confirms it.
2. `node sdk/cli.ts verify-batch manifest.json --out results.json` (or `npm run -s carbonlei -- verify-batch …`). Every proof is verified with the same checks as `verify --json`, at one block read once at the start, so all verdicts describe the same chain state (`pinnedBlock` in the results file). At most `--concurrency` proofs (default 2, at most 4) are verified at once, to stay within public RPC limits. The head-age limit still applies to that block: split a batch that takes more than about five minutes. Exit code: 3 if a verification could not run, else 1 if any proof is INVALID, 2 if any is CONTESTED, 4 if any is INCOMPLETE, else 0.
3. `node sdk/cli.ts portfolio results.json --year 2026 --price 82.32 --csv worksheet.csv` prints the plan (`--json` or `--out` for the [schema](schemas/portfolio.schema.json)'s JSON) and writes the worksheet. Without `--price` the CLI gives no € figures (2026 goods are priced per quarter of importation; the SDK's `quarterPrices` option takes one price per quarter). `--benchmarks map.json` (`{"TW:7318": "1.364"}`, optional `"cbamFactor"`) deducts the free-allocation adjustment on default lines; `--defaults map.json` adds default values for goods the SDK does not list (only CN 7318 from Taiwan is built in).
4. Review every default line's reason (no proof, a verdict other than VALID, check 5 not bound to your EORI, a CN code or origin the credential does not cover, more tonnes declared than were claimed on the ledger). One ledger claim (one proof's batch) may be split over several lines; their tonnes are counted together against the claim in manifest order, and a line that would exceed it is planned on the default ("… remain (already used by …)"). The plan's notes list every claim that is on more than one line.
5. Before each quarter end and before filing, re-check what you accepted: `node sdk/cli.ts recheck results.json --proofs <proof folder> --out recheck.json`, then `node sdk/cli.ts portfolio results.json --year 2026 --recheck recheck.json`. A line the re-check says needs action (revoked, superseded, CONTESTED, claim gone) is then planned on the default, with the re-check as its reason ([Importer re-check](#importer-re-check-from-acceptance-to-filing)). The results file records each proof's SHA-256 (`proofSha256`): `recheck` re-verifies only a file with those exact bytes, and a file of the same name with other bytes is RECHECK_FAILED, so keep the verified proof files unchanged.

`portfolio` plans from the verdicts in the results file and does not verify any proof again; its output says so ("Verdicts: taken from the input file …"). Keep results files where only you can change them.

| Benchmark for CN 7318 (demo tab) | Column B 1.364 tCO2e/t for carbon steel, route (C) BF/BOF, the route IR 2026/1740 marks on Taiwan's carbon-steel CN 7318 lines (D 0.475, E 0.066; stainless 1.154 for 2026–27, 1.116 for 2028–30; Column A 0.038) | IR (EU) 2025/2620, Annex point 5.3 [4]; IR (EU) 2026/1740, Annex I, Taiwan [1] |

**Parameters with no built-in value.** The SDK does not choose a benchmark: the right Column B value depends on the 8-digit CN code, the steel grade and the origin's default route, so the caller passes it (the demo tab passes 1.364 for CN 7318 carbon steel, route C). A line declared on a verified value is adjusted only if the caller passes the operator's verified SEFA, which the credential does not carry; otherwise it stays gross and the plan says so. The carbon price paid in the country of origin (Art 9) is not deducted from the totals; `--art9-ref-price <EUR>` adds a separate `art9` block with the deduction of lines whose check 10 passed, on the Commission's draft rules (not adopted, illustrative; [SECURITY §4.4](SECURITY.md#44-check-10-carbon-price-paid-draft-art-9-rules)). Option (b) of Art 22(2), last year's surrender, is not modelled. A marked-up default can differ from the Registry's in the last decimal.

**Rounding.** Quantities exact to the kilogram; each line's tCO2e rounded half up to 3 decimals and totals summed from the rounded lines; certificates rounded up to whole certificates (a purchase plan, not a Registry rule); € = Σ line obligation × price, rounded to the cent. The full list is in the plan's `rounding` field.

## Importer re-check: from acceptance to filing

A value accepted today can stop being usable later: the verification body revokes the report or a revision supersedes it, the watcher revokes the auditor or suspends the body within 24 hours after the registration (the report becomes CONTESTED), the registering address is rotated away, or the claim is no longer on the ledger. The first CBAM declaration, for 2026 imports, is due by 30 September 2027 (Reg. (EU) 2023/956 Art 6(1) as amended by Reg. (EU) 2025/2083; [CLIMATE_IMPACT](CLIMATE_IMPACT.md) source 20), so a value accepted in 2026 must hold for months. `carbonlei recheck` replaces "re-run the check later": it re-verifies every accepted record at one pinned block and says what changed since acceptance and what to do. Code: [`sdk/recheck.ts`](../sdk/recheck.ts) (no file system, also used by the hosted page), [`sdk/recheck-files.ts`](../sdk/recheck-files.ts); schema: [`schemas/recheck.schema.json`](schemas/recheck.schema.json).

**Procedure**

1. Keep what you accept: the Buyer tab's "Download verification record (JSON)" (it now carries the proof), or the `verify-batch` results file with the proof files, or the verdict of `verify --json` / POST `/v1/verify` stored with its proof as `{ "proof", "verdict", "eori", "acceptedAt" }`. Keep them in one folder.
2. `node sdk/cli.ts recheck <folder> --out recheck.json` (`--json` prints the JSON; `--proofs <folder>` tells it where a results file's proofs are). A record without its proof is re-checked against the ledger only (the report's registration, revocation, supersession and validity, by its credential ID) and says so.
3. Schedule it daily until you file, for example with [`examples/importer-recheck.yml`](examples/importer-recheck.yml) (GitHub Actions; copy it into your own repository). `--webhook <URL>` or `CARBONLEI_RECHECK_WEBHOOK_URL` POSTs the changed lines only (id, credential ID, change, severity, verdicts, action); never the EORI, the proof or the disclosed values. The URL is never printed, because it often carries a token.
4. Act on each line: **action** → declare the line on the default value (with the year's mark-up) until the supplier sends a new proof that verifies VALID; for a supersession, ask the supplier for the proof of the new credential; for a CONTESTED line, a person reviews it ([Incident procedures](#incident-procedures)). **info** → nothing to change. **error** → the accepted value is not confirmed: fix the cause (usually the RPC nodes) and run again. `portfolio --recheck recheck.json` applies the action lines to the plan.
5. Keep `recheck.json` with the record of the accepted value.

| Change | Severity | When |
|---|---|---|
| `REPORT_REVOKED` | action | The body revoked the report (check 4), or it was revoked after the claim (check 5) |
| `REPORT_SUPERSEDED` | action | A revision replaced the report before your claim (judged at the claim time when bound to your EORI); in ledger-only mode, any supersession (fail closed: the claim time is unknown) |
| `AUDITOR_REVOKED_OR_BODY_SUSPENDED`, `ADDRESS_ROTATED`, `REVOCATION_CONTESTED`, `CONTESTED` | action | The verdict is now CONTESTED, with the CONTESTED rule that fired |
| `CLAIM_NOT_ON_LEDGER`, `SHIPMENT_CHANGED`, `REPORT_NOT_ON_LEDGER`, `REPORT_EXPIRED`, `AUTHORITY_INVALID`, `ANCHOR_INVALID`, `NOW_INVALID`, `NOW_INCOMPLETE` | action | Check 5, 4, 7 or 6 fails, or another check, or some checks could not run |
| `BECAME_FINAL` | info | Still VALID; accepted while the 24 h window was open, which has since closed with no event. The report can still be revoked or superseded later |
| `WINDOW_OPEN` | info | Still VALID; the window is still open: re-check after it closes |
| `IMPROVED`, `EXPIRED_SINCE` | info | A line that was not VALID now is (review before using it); ledger-only: past validity, which does not matter for a shipment claimed before |
| `UNCHANGED`, `STILL_NOT_VALID` | none | Nothing to change |
| `RECHECK_FAILED` | error | Unreadable record, record from another chain or registry, RPC failure |

Exit codes: 3 if any record could not be re-checked or the webhook could not be delivered, else 1 if any line needs action, else 0. A scheduler therefore fails the run, and alerts, whenever a person has to look.

## Supplier allocations: one report, many buyers

A supplier with one verified report usually sells to several EU importers, and each asks for the same data. The supplier keeps an **allocation book** (a local JSON file; [example](examples/allocation-book.json)) and answers every buyer from it. Code: [`sdk/allocation.ts`](../sdk/allocation.ts) (no file system, also used by the hosted Supplier tab), [`sdk/allocation-files.ts`](../sdk/allocation-files.ts).

**The book.** `credential` (path of the `carbonlei issue` output, relative to the book; or pass `--credential`), optional `defaultFields`, `importers` (`id`, `name`, `eori`, optional `fields`) and `batches` (`batchId`, `importer` id, `importerSalt`, `quantityTonnes`, `shipmentDate`, `status`: `planned` or `claimed`, optional `fields`). The required fields of a proof are always added to a buyer's list. Use a fresh 32-byte salt per batch (`newSalt()` in the SDK): two batches with the same salt for the same importer are linkable on-chain, and `allocate` warns (`SALT_REUSED`).

**Procedure**

1. Before claiming, run `node sdk/cli.ts allocate book.json`. Planned batches are checked in book order against the remaining tonnes; a batch the contract would refuse is `OVER_ALLOCATED` (it would revert with `ExceedsVerifiedTonnage`, as in attack 2b). Reduce it, move it to another report, or plan it on the default value.
2. Send each claim from the supplier wallet (`claimShipment`, arguments from `claimArgsOf` in the SDK; the CLI sends no transaction). Mark the batch `claimed` in the book.
3. Run `allocate` again. Each claimed batch must be `CLAIMED`. `CLAIM_MISSING` (not sent, reverted, or another batch ID or salt), `QUANTITY_MISMATCH` or `IMPORTER_MISMATCH` must be fixed before any proof goes out: the proof would fail check 5 for that buyer. A claim is final on-chain: if it was sent with the wrong importer or quantity, the batch ID is used up; record what happened and claim the remaining goods under a new batch ID. `UNKNOWN_CLAIM` (an on-chain claim of this report that the book does not list, found from the `ShipmentClaimed` events) means the book is incomplete or the supplier key was used by someone else ([SECURITY T33](SECURITY.md#supplier-allocation-book)). `--no-events` skips the event search (faster on public nodes) and reports the ledger difference as `UNACCOUNTED_KG` instead.
4. `node sdk/cli.ts present-batch book.json --check --evidence-from <a proof of this credential> --out share-pack/` writes one proof per claimed batch (`<importer id>--<batch id>--<hash>.proof.json`; the 10-digit hash of both full ids keeps names apart that would otherwise read the same) and `share-pack.json` (who gets which file and which fields; no EORI, no salt). It refuses to overwrite any file, so write each pack to a new folder. `--check` skips batches whose claim does not match the chain. `--evidence-from` copies the anchor, authority, report-extract and precursor evidence from an existing proof, which checks 6–9 need.
5. Send each buyer only its own file. Each buyer verifies it with its own EORI (`verify --eori`); another buyer's file fails check 5.
6. Keep the book with the credential, under the same protection as the supplier wallet's records: it holds every buyer's EORI and the salts that link them to the on-chain commitments.

Exit codes: `allocate` 0 when nothing needs action, 1 when something does, 3 when it could not run (bad book, unreadable credential, RPC failure; with `--json`, an `{ "error" }` object). `present-batch` 0 when at least one proof was written, 1 when none, 3 when `--check` could not read the registry.

| Issue | Severity | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `CLAIM_MISSING` | action | Book says claimed; no claim on-chain for that batch key |
| `QUANTITY_MISMATCH`, `IMPORTER_MISMATCH` | action | On-chain claim differs from the book in quantity or importer commitment |
| `OVER_ALLOCATED` | action | Planned batch exceeds the tonnes left after the planned batches before it |
| `REPORT_NOT_CLAIMABLE`, `REPORT_REVOKED` | action | The report takes no claims (revoked, replaced, expired, not registered); claimed batches of a revoked report fail check 4 |
| `UNKNOWN_CLAIM` | action | On-chain claim of this report not in the book |
| `UNACCOUNTED_KG` | warning (action with events) | Ledger claims exceed the book's; includes claims carried over from a replaced report |
| `BOOK_STALE`, `SALT_REUSED` | warning | Book says planned but the claim is on-chain; two batches share a salt |

## REST API (`carbonlei serve`): deployment and security

`node sdk/cli.ts serve [--port 8787] [--host 127.0.0.1]` serves the same SDK calls over HTTP, for an ERP or compliance tool: POST `/v1/verify`, `/v1/verify-batch`, `/v1/portfolio`, `/v1/recheck`; GET `/v1/health` (chain ID, head block and its age, RPC node hosts, limits) and `/openapi.json` ([`schemas/openapi.json`](schemas/openapi.json), OpenAPI 3.1, hand-written). A verdict from `/v1/verify` equals `carbonlei verify --json` (tested); the header `X-CarbonLEI-Exit` carries the CLI exit code. Code: [`sdk/server.ts`](../sdk/server.ts), `node:http` only, no new dependency. It is a prototype.

**Security caveats (read before deploying)**

- **No authentication, no TLS.** Anyone who can reach the port can use it. It binds 127.0.0.1 by default and prints a warning when bound to any other address. Expose it only behind your own API gateway or reverse proxy, which adds authentication, TLS and rate limits.
- **Denial of service.** Each verification sends tens of RPC requests. Limits: 1,000,000-byte bodies (413), `Content-Type: application/json` only (415), 10 s to upload the body (408, connection closed; the body is read before a verification slot is taken, at most 32 at once), 60 s per request after that (504), 4 POST requests at once (503 with `Retry-After`), 50 lines per verify-batch, 500 records per re-check; node:http header and request timeouts against slow clients. A timed-out verification keeps its slot until it ends (it cannot be cancelled midway), so the cap holds. Tune with `--max-body`, `--body-timeout`, `--timeout`, `--max-concurrent`; the RPC provider's own rate limits still apply.
- **Verdicts bound to the server.** `/v1/verify-batch` and `/v1/recheck` return their document with `binding`, an HMAC-SHA256 the server computes with its key. `/v1/portfolio` plans `results` (and `recheck`) only when that binding is intact, so pass the documents back unchanged; anything else is refused with 422. Set `CARBONLEI_API_HMAC_KEY` (at least 32 bytes, kept like any secret) to keep bound documents valid across restarts; without it the server makes a new random key at each start. To plan from verdicts you made elsewhere (for example `carbonlei verify-batch` output), send `"trustCallerVerdicts": true`: the plan is computed, and its `verdictSource` and first note say "computed from caller-supplied verdicts, not verified by this server". `GET /v1/health` states the rule under `verdictBinding`.
- **Personal data in logs.** One JSON line per request on stderr: request id, method, path, status, duration, body size, outcome (verdict or counts) and whether an EORI was given. Never the body, the EORI (not even hashed: an EORI is short and structured enough that an unsalted hash could be reversed by trying them), the client address or the query string. URLs inside error messages are cut to their origin, so an RPC API key in a URL path is not logged or returned.
- **Files.** Evidence bundles a proof refers to are read only from `--evidence-dir` (default `demo/public`), after resolving symbolic links, at most 5 MB each; a path outside it is refused. A request body that repeats a JSON key is refused (400), as check 0 refuses a proof that does.
- **CORS** is off: no browser page on another origin can call it, unless `--cors-origin <origin>` allows exactly one.
- It never sends a transaction and holds no key. Threats: [SECURITY › HTTP API](SECURITY.md#14-http-api-carbonlei-serve).

## Not built

- ERP or EDI connectors: the inputs are an Excel template and JSON files ([`sdk/cli.ts`](../sdk/cli.ts)); an ERP can call the prototype REST API (`carbonlei serve`), but no ERP-specific connector exists. [`examples/erp-integration/`](../examples/erp-integration/) shows the flow with a generic ERP CSV export: map to `/v1/verify-batch`, check the HMAC binding, plan with `/v1/portfolio`, write a CSV back (not a CBAM Registry filing; no SAP or Oracle connector).
- A CBAM Registry XML export: the Commission has published no data structure for the definitive-period declaration (its Registry module opens in 2027), and its one published XML schema, the transitional `QReport` 18.30, is archived and covers only 2023–2025. [REGISTRY_EXPORT](REGISTRY_EXPORT.md) lists the official sources and maps CarbonLEI's fields to the Commission's communication template; `carbonlei export-communication` writes that mapping as a CSV of the template's `Summary_Communication` fields from a verified proof (no .xlsx; not validated by the Commission, not a Registry submission).
- A hosted alerting service: `carbonlei recheck --webhook` runs on the importer's own scheduler and POSTs to the importer's own endpoint; CarbonLEI runs nothing that watches on an importer's behalf.
- A connection to any PACT network: the export is field-compatible JSON ([PACT_MAPPING](PACT_MAPPING.md); [ADOPTION §8](ADOPTION.md#8-current-status-by-area)).
- Witness operation beyond the local containers ([PILOT §3](PILOT.md#3-what-runs-where)).
- Custodial wallets: each party signs with its own key ([PILOT §2](PILOT.md#2-roles-and-what-each-must-provide)).
- Gas sponsorship: each sender pays its own gas ([PILOT §6](PILOT.md#6-costs-test-network-and-production-chain)).
