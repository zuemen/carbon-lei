# FAQ for reviewers

This document holds the reviewer questions not answered in the README; questions are numbered as in the full list, so the numbering skips.

The full question list; Q1, Q2, Q5, Q6, Q7 and Q21 are answered in the [README](../README.md#six-questions-reviewers-ask), and the other seventeen are answered here under their original numbers.

**Q3. Can one installation obtain two reports and double its claimable tonnage?**
Within one report, no: claims can never exceed the report's verified tonnage, and an invariant fuzz test checks this for any claim order. Across reports, the rules allow one verification report per installation and reporting period, plus a revised report at the operator's request (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/2551, Annex II, point 2.17.3). The contract enforces that rule across verification bodies with a two-level key. At report level, an installation and reporting period stay bound to the first report registered for them: revoking it does not free the period, and a new report must name the report it supersedes, or it reverts with `PeriodAlreadyCovered`. Only the original issuing body may supersede its own report, unless that body has been suspended or its accreditation has expired. At credential level (installation, CN code, production route, reporting period), a revision of the same credential carries over the tonnes already claimed; a revision whose `verifiedKg` is below them reverts with `SupersedeOverClaimed`. When another body takes over a period from a suspended or expired body, its report must start in a credential scope with no claims yet; the old report stops being valid, and its credential scope keeps the tonnes already claimed and accepts no new claims until a revision restores it, so claimed tonnes are never reset. The invariant fuzz test includes revoke, revise and take-over actions. SECURITY.md (threat T19) describes the control.

**Q4. Can the supplier's on-chain address be linked back to its LEI? What do the salts protect?**
Partly, yes. The `supplier` and `verifier` fields are plain EVM addresses, so all reports and claims from one address can be linked to each other, and anyone who learns which company uses an address can link it to that company's LEI. The 256-bit salts protect the supplier's LEI, the importer's EORI and the batch ID: nobody can hash a list of all known LEIs or EORIs and match it against the chain. While few companies use the system, tonnages compared with public trade statistics could also point to a company. Both risks are listed in SECURITY.md §7. Roadmap: address rotation and zero-knowledge claims.
Two exceptions. The verification body's LEI is hashed without a salt, because the allowlist of accredited bodies is meant to be public. And the two scope keys that enforce one report per installation and reporting period (and one credential per installation, CN code, production route and period) are not salted, because the contract must match them across verification bodies. Anyone who knows an installation ID can therefore confirm that the installation has a report registered for a given period, and for which CN codes and routes (SECURITY.md §7).

**Q8. Where is AI used, and if it is not, why?**
During development, an AI assistant helped with code and text; the team reviewed and tested all of it (see [AI usage disclosure](../README.md#ai-usage-disclosure)). None of the checks uses AI: all eight checks, seven verification checks and one advisory reconciliation check (check 8, rule-based), are deterministic and give the same result every time.
Check 8 (rule-based reconciliation of report and credential, rule version `carbonlei-reconciliation/1`) is deterministic, so anyone can rerun it and get the same hashes that the credential carries; it only warns and never approves or rejects a report. Roadmap: an optional local, fixed-weight model for semantic checks (for example free-text accreditation scopes), with weights hash and runtime version in the attestation.

**Q9. Everything is fictional, even the GLEIF root. Would real vLEIs work?**
The companies, people and LEIs are fictional (prefix ZZZZ, not found in the GLEIF API), and the GLEIF root is simulated locally with test keys. The credential flow runs on the open-source KERI stack: `verifier/` starts KERIA agents and witnesses locally, creates 8 agents, issues the QVI, Legal Entity, accreditation and ECR credentials with signify-ts, anchors the demo credential in the lead auditor's KEL and exports the evidence that checks 6 and 7 verify. Using real vLEIs would mean configuring the production GLEIF root of trust and obtaining credentials from a QVI. The verification steps are designed to stay the same, but we have not tested them with real vLEIs. Two parts are our own design and are not issued by anyone today: the CBAM accreditation credential from a NAB, and the "CBAM Lead Auditor" ECR role. A pilot with a real QVI is on the roadmap.

**Q10. What happens when one verification report covers several products or processes?**
One credential covers exactly one combination of installation, CN code, production route and reporting period. A verification report that covers several products or processes leads to several credentials that share one `verificationReportId`. Each credential has its own tonnage ledger.

**Q11. How much carbon is actually reduced?**
CarbonLEI's contribution is the integrity of verified emissions data: each verified tonne of goods can be claimed only once within this deployment, per credential, across importers (this does not show that the physical goods are unique; a second report for the same installation and reporting period reverts with `PeriodAlreadyCovered` unless it names the report it supersedes), and software can check who signed a value and whether they were authorised at the block that registered the report, as far as the watcher has synced revocations, with a 24-hour CONTESTED window. That is environmental transparency and carbon tracking. Its effect has three layers. (1) Declaration accuracy: verified actual values replace marked-up default values, and the ledger stops a low verified value from being applied to more tonnes of goods than were verified. (2) Price signal: the declared-emissions gap times the certificate price, about €97 per tonne in 2026 and about €142 per tonne from 2028, before free-allocation adjustment (illustrative; price held constant at the Q3 2026 level of €82.32, published 5 October 2026); who captures that value is a commercial matter between buyer and producer. (3) Physical change: where that value reaches the producer, it is an incentive to use lower-carbon steel, but free allocation offsets much of it until about 2030, and it is complete only in 2034. We found no public evidence of fastener makers switching steel because of CBAM, so we give no number: we do not claim a quantified physical reduction. CarbonLEI's own footprint is in [Footprint of CarbonLEI](CLIMATE_IMPACT.md#footprint-of-carbonlei).

**Q12. The CBAM Registry already handles verification reports centrally. Why an on-chain ledger?**
Inside the Registry, two things are already handled: proof of the verification body's representation is submitted when its EU Login account is requested, and only one verification report may cover an installation and reporting period. CarbonLEI does not duplicate or replace that; it does not submit to the Registry, and CBAM does not recognise vLEIs. Even with a single importer, the on-chain claim record gives that importer a timestamped public record of which verified report and how many tonnes its shipment used, which it can show if its declaration is reviewed after the fact (Article 19 of the CBAM Regulation). Protection across importers grows with adoption. See [Where CarbonLEI sits, and why a ledger](../README.md#where-carbonlei-sits-and-why-a-ledger).
What CarbonLEI covers lies outside the Registry and across importers. The Registry is not open to the general public, and a registered operator can share one verified value with any number of declarants; we found no public mechanism that caps the total tonnage declared against one verified value across importers. CarbonLEI lets downstream buyers, banks, product passports and recipients of the e-signed PDF copy in 2027 check the verification body's authority by machine, and it adds a voluntary cap, across importers, on the tonnes claimed against one verified value.

**Q13. Can a Taiwanese screw maker get a vLEI today?**
It does not need one. Only the verification body and its lead auditor need vLEI credentials (and an accreditation body, if it issues the accreditation credential itself). The supplier needs no vLEI: its LEI identifies it, and the Commission's guidance for non-EU operators lists the LEI as an accepted identifier for operators from countries such as Taiwan [1]. Besides the LEI, it needs an EVM wallet to claim shipments and the signed credential its verification body issues. The demo also issues the supplier a Legal Entity vLEI, marked optional. For context: on 2026-09-23 the GLEIF website listed 8 qualified vLEI issuers (QVIs); GLEIF does not publish how many vLEIs have been issued.

**Q14. How is a batch ID bound to the physical goods?**
In this prototype it is not; this is a known limitation. The supplier chooses the batch ID. The contract makes each batch claimable once and caps all claims at the verified tonnage, so a new batch ID cannot create tonnes beyond the report, but it cannot prove the goods came from that installation. Roadmap: bind the batch to the customs declaration or to mill heat numbers.

**Q15. If we redeploy and rerun, will the importer's check still pass after the report expires?**
Yes, by design, for the importer the shipment was declared to. Validity is judged at the shipment's claim time when the importer verifies with its own EORI (check 5 binds the batch to it): the on-chain check then uses `isValidAt(reportKey, t)` with `t` set to the shipment's `claimedAt`, so a shipment claimed before `validUntil` still verifies when the importer declares the following year. Anyone else, including a verifier that gives no EORI, gets the report judged at the latest block, so an expired or superseded report fails for them. Revocation still applies immediately: a revoked report marks all its shipments invalid. When a verification body's institution record moves to a new current address, the old address is invalidated for new registrations immediately, but a registered report's validity is never recomputed against later authority changes, so older reports stay valid. Contract tests cover expiry, rotation and the claim-time check. A redeployment gives new contract addresses and a new EIP-712 domain, so credentials must be re-signed, or the old contracts kept read-only (SECURITY.md §5). This repository did that once: the earlier deployment is kept in `contracts/deployments/archive/`, and the demo credential was issued again for the current contracts.

**Q16. Registered operators can already share verified values with every declarant through the Registry. Against that baseline, how many extra tonnes do you save?**
Inside the Registry, CarbonLEI adds transparency, not tonnes: machine-checkable authority and a public claim record. There the verified value already replaces the default value, so we count no extra tonnes for CarbonLEI ([CLIMATE_IMPACT §4.4](CLIMATE_IMPACT.md#44-attribution-what-carbonlei-itself-adds)).
CarbonLEI's effect lies outside the Registry (downstream buyers, banks, product passports, the e-signed PDF copy in 2027) and across importers: the verification body's authority stays machine-checkable wherever the report travels, and the tonnage ledger adds a voluntary cap on the tonnes claimed against one verified value. Its size is unknown: we cannot estimate how many verified values will travel outside the Registry. We do not quantify the effect of the tonnage ledger; in the demo, it rejects a 400 t claim for a second importer when 300 t of the 500 t remain (a dry run against the live Sepolia contract that returns `ExceedsVerifiedTonnage`).

**Q17. How does lowering the CBAM burden count as a climate benefit? The mark-up is deliberately conservative.**
We do not count a lower CBAM bill as a climate benefit. The mark-up exists to push operators to provide verified data, and CarbonLEI works in the same direction: it makes verified values checkable and single-use. A low verified value can replace the default only for the tonnes it actually covers, so real emissions do not drop out of declarations. Accurate declarations then let lower-carbon production earn a price advantage. The causal chain, with the CBAM articles it rests on and its limits: [How it connects to climate outcomes](CLIMATE_IMPACT.md#how-it-connects-to-climate-outcomes).

**Q18. How many verification reports will exist in Taiwan in 2027 for importers to use?**
We do not know. In the Commission's state of play of 29 September 2026, 24 EU/EEA national accreditation bodies had agreed to offer CBAM accreditation, but only 5 were accepting applications from verifiers outside the EU, and the first verification reports are expected in January 2027 [2]. Early volume will be small. That is why we built one corridor end to end (Taiwan fasteners into the EU) instead of claiming wide coverage.

**Q19. If you remove the AI (or rule) layer, what does the system lose?**
The seven verification checks do not depend on it and keep working.
What it loses is check 8: the auditable record that the verification report and the signed credential agree, and the regulatory checks (accreditation scope, first-year site visit, assurance level, materiality, dates). The input, output and rule-version hashes of that comparison sit in the credential core, which the `credSAID` covers; the lead auditor anchors that `credSAID` and the verification body signs it. Without this layer, those signatures no longer cover a machine-checkable comparison between report and credential.

**Q20. Why not just use a signed database?**
A signed database works when every party trusts whoever runs it. Here the writers are several verification bodies and suppliers, and the readers are importers in different member states. The part that needs a record none of them controls is the tonnage ledger shared across importers: in the demo, a 400 t claim for a second importer is rejected because only 300 t of the 500 t remain. What is not decentralised yet is the allowlist itself (see Q1 in the [README](../README.md#six-questions-reviewers-ask)). See [Where CarbonLEI sits, and why a ledger](../README.md#where-carbonlei-sits-and-why-a-ledger).
A second part needs it too: for reports used outside the CBAM Registry, the rule of one verification report per installation and reporting period is enforced across verification bodies. No single body's database sees the other bodies' reports; the contract rejects a second report for the same installation and period with `PeriodAlreadyCovered` unless it names the report it supersedes, and only the original issuing body may supersede its own report unless that body has been suspended or its accreditation has expired.

**Q22. Why not eIDAS signatures?**
They solve part of the problem, and CarbonLEI does not replace them. In the EU a qualified electronic signature has the legal effect of a handwritten one (Regulation (EU) No 910/2014, Art. 25(2) [3]). Its certificate names the signatory and may name their organisation, including its LEI (ETSI EN 319 412-2, NAT-4.2.4-6; EN 319 412-1, clause 5.1.4 [5]), and validation checks that the certificate was valid at the time of signing (Art. 32 [3]). A PAdES signature can also carry a signer role, either claimed by the signer or certified by an attribute authority (ETSI EN 319 122-1, clause 5.2.6.1 [5]).
What a standard qualified certificate does not carry is what the CBAM rules ask for: that the person who authenticates the verification report is "duly authorised to do so by the verifier" (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/2551, Annex II, point 2.15.2 [4]), and that the verifier was "duly accredited at the time" (Commission verification guidance, p.123). An electronic attestation of attributes could express this, since eIDAS lists "powers and mandates to represent" (Annex VI [3]), but we found none issued for CBAM verifiers, and the European Business Wallet proposal, which covers mandates, is still in the legislative process (2025/0358(COD) [7]). The guidance calls the 2027 copy an "electronically signed PDF" (p.68) and does not say whether the signature is qualified or advanced, or who applies it.
Neither eIDAS signatures nor attestations track the tonnes claimed across importers; that is what the ledger adds. The two approaches can be combined: GLEIF says it is "working toward the acceptance of the vLEI as an electronic attestation of attributes" (September 2024 [6]); the vLEI is not today an attestation under eIDAS, and CBAM does not recognise vLEIs (Q12).

**Q23. What if a report is revoked after the importer has declared?**
What CarbonLEI shows: the verification body revokes with `revokeReport`. The revocation is public (a `ReportRevoked` event with its block time, and `revokedAt` in the report record) and cannot be undone. From then on, every check of a proof for that report fails check 4 with `REPORT_INVALID/REVOKED` and the verdict is INVALID (CLI exit code 1), whenever the shipment was claimed: unlike expiry or supersession, a revocation is not judged at the claim time (Q15), and `shipmentStatus` returns the shipment's report as no longer valid. The exception is a revocation that looks like a stolen key's: if the address that sent it was rotated away within 24 hours after it, or was rotated in and away again within 30 days, the verdict is CONTESTED (exit code 2) and a person decides ([OPERATE, Incident procedures](OPERATE.md#incident-procedures), step 1).
What it does not show: the verdict's detail does not print when the report was revoked. The importer can read that from the `ReportRevoked` event and compare it with the block time of its own claim transaction, which stays on-chain unchanged; the verification record and importer's file it saved when it accepted the value (ADOPTION §2.1) are unsigned copies of the checks at that time. CarbonLEI sends no notification: the importer sees a revocation only when it verifies again or reads the event. What the importer must then do under CBAM is set by the CBAM rules and its competent authority, not by CarbonLEI, and we do not describe a procedure here; a CBAM declaration can be reviewed after submission (Regulation (EU) 2023/956, Art 19, ADOPTION §1.1).
