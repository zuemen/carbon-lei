# FAQ for reviewers

This document holds the reviewer questions not answered in the README; questions are numbered as in the full list, so the numbering skips.

The full question list; Q1, Q2, Q5, Q6, Q7 and Q21 are answered in the [README](../README.md#six-questions-reviewers-ask), and the other twenty-two are answered here under their original numbers.

**Q3. Can one installation obtain two reports and double its claimable tonnage?**
Within one report, no: claims can never exceed the report's verified tonnage, and an invariant fuzz test checks this for any claim order; bounded Halmos checks prove it for every quantity in fixed sequences of up to five transactions, such as three claims, or a claim, a revision and two more claims (not for all sequences; [SECURITY §9.2](SECURITY.md#92-symbolic-and-bounded-checking)). A bounded model check of an abstract protocol model in Maude finds no reachable state, on small instances, where the tonnes accepted by importers for one product exceed the verified tonnes, across revisions and a second slot label ([SECURITY §9.3](SECURITY.md#93-protocol-model-maude)). Across reports, the rules allow one verification report per installation and reporting period, plus a revised report at the operator's request (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/2551, Annex II, point 2.17.3). The contract enforces that rule across verification bodies with a two-level key. At report level, an installation and reporting period stay bound to the first report registered for them: revoking it does not free the period, and a new report must name the report it supersedes, or it reverts with `PeriodAlreadyCovered`. Only the original issuing body may supersede its own report, unless that body has been suspended or its accreditation has expired. At credential level (installation, CN code, production route, reporting period), a revision of the same credential carries over the tonnes already claimed; a revision whose `verifiedKg` is below them reverts with `SupersedeOverClaimed`. When another body takes over a period from a suspended or expired body, its report must start in a credential scope with no claims yet; the old report stops being valid, and its credential scope keeps the tonnes already claimed and accepts no new claims until a revision restores it, so claimed tonnes are never reset. The invariant fuzz test includes revoke, revise and take-over actions. SECURITY.md (threat T19) describes the control.

**Q4. Can the supplier's on-chain address be linked back to its LEI? What do the salts protect?**
Partly, yes. The `supplier` and `verifier` fields are plain EVM addresses, so all reports and claims from one address can be linked to each other, and anyone who learns which company uses an address can link it to that company's LEI. The 256-bit salts protect the supplier's LEI, the importer's EORI and the batch ID: nobody can hash a list of all known LEIs or EORIs and match it against the chain. While few companies use the system, tonnages compared with public trade statistics could also point to a company. Both risks are listed in SECURITY.md §7. Roadmap: address rotation and zero-knowledge claims.
Two exceptions. The verification body's LEI is hashed without a salt, because the allowlist of accredited bodies is meant to be public. And the two scope keys that enforce one report per installation and reporting period (and one credential per installation, CN code, production route and period) are not salted, because the contract must match them across verification bodies. Anyone who knows an installation ID can therefore confirm that the installation has a report registered for a given period, and for which CN codes and routes (SECURITY.md §7).

**Q8. Where is AI used, and if it is not, why?**
During development, an AI assistant helped with code and text; the team reviewed and tested all of it (see [AI usage disclosure](../README.md#ai-usage-disclosure)). None of the checks (0–10) uses AI; all are deterministic rules and give the same result every time. The Sepolia demo proof runs eight (seven verification checks and the advisory, rule-based check 8); checks 9 and 10 apply only to proofs with a precursor or carbon-price statement.
Check 8 (rule-based reconciliation of report and credential, rule version `carbonlei-reconciliation/1`) is deterministic, so anyone can rerun it and get the same hashes that the credential carries; its twelve rules only warn and never approve or reject a report (its sub-rules on the report itself can fail it, and for a credential that carries the hashes an extract that is missing or no longer matches them makes the result INCOMPLETE). Roadmap: an optional local, fixed-weight model for semantic checks (for example free-text accreditation scopes), with weights hash and runtime version in the attestation.

**Q9. Everything is fictional, even the GLEIF root. Would real vLEIs work?**
The companies, people and LEIs are fictional (prefix ZZZZ, not found in the GLEIF API), and the GLEIF root is simulated locally with test keys. The credential flow runs on the open-source KERI stack: `verifier/` starts KERIA agents and witnesses locally, creates 8 agents, issues the QVI, Legal Entity, accreditation and ECR credentials with signify-ts, anchors the demo credential in the lead auditor's KEL and exports the evidence that checks 6 and 7 verify. Using real vLEIs would mean configuring the production GLEIF root of trust and obtaining credentials from a QVI. The verification steps are designed to stay the same, but we have not tested them with real vLEIs. Two parts are our own design and are not issued by anyone today: the CBAM accreditation credential from a NAB, and the "CBAM Lead Auditor" ECR role. A pilot with a real QVI is on the roadmap.

**Q10. What happens when one verification report covers several products or processes?**
One credential covers exactly one combination of installation, CN code, production route and reporting period. A verification report that covers several products or processes leads to several credentials that share one `verificationReportId`. Each credential has its own tonnage ledger.

**Q11. How much carbon is actually reduced?**
CarbonLEI's contribution is the integrity of verified emissions data: each verified tonne of goods can be claimed only once within this deployment, per credential, across importers (this does not show that the physical goods are unique; a second report for the same installation and reporting period reverts with `PeriodAlreadyCovered` unless it names the report it supersedes), and software can check who signed a value and whether they were authorised at the block that registered the report, as far as the watcher has synced revocations, with a 24-hour CONTESTED window. That is environmental transparency and carbon tracking. Its effect has three layers. (1) Declaration accuracy: verified actual values replace marked-up default values, and the ledger stops a low verified value from being applied to more tonnes of goods than were verified. (2) Price signal: the declared-emissions gap times the certificate price, about €97 per tonne in 2026 and about €142 per tonne from 2028, before free-allocation adjustment (illustrative; price held constant at the Q3 2026 level of €82.32, published 5 October 2026); who captures that value is a commercial matter between buyer and producer. (3) Physical change: where that value reaches the producer, it is an incentive to use lower-carbon steel, but free allocation offsets much of it until about 2030, and it is complete only in 2034. We found no public evidence of fastener makers switching steel because of CBAM, so we give no number: we do not claim a quantified physical reduction. CarbonLEI's own footprint is in [Footprint of CarbonLEI](CLIMATE_IMPACT.md#footprint-of-carbonlei).

**Q12. The CBAM Registry already handles verification reports centrally. Why an on-chain ledger?**
Inside the Registry, two things are already handled: proof of the verification body's representation is submitted when its EU Login account is requested, and only one verification report may cover an installation and reporting period. CarbonLEI does not duplicate or replace that; it does not submit to the Registry, and CBAM does not recognise vLEIs. Even with a single importer, the on-chain claim record gives that importer a timestamped public record of which verified report and how many tonnes its shipment used, which it can show if its declaration is reviewed after the fact (Article 19 of the CBAM Regulation). Protection across importers grows with adoption. See [Where CarbonLEI sits, and why a ledger](../README.md#where-carbonlei-sits-and-why-a-ledger).
What CarbonLEI covers lies outside the Registry and across importers. The Registry is not open to the general public, and a registered operator can share one verified value with any number of declarants; we found no public mechanism that caps the total tonnage declared against one verified value across importers. CarbonLEI lets downstream buyers, banks, product passports and recipients of the e-signed PDF copy in 2027 check the verification body's authority by machine, and it adds a voluntary cap, across importers, on the tonnes claimed against one verified value.

**Q13. Can a Taiwanese screw maker get a vLEI today?**
It does not need one. Only the verification body and its lead auditor need vLEI credentials (and an accreditation body, if it issues the accreditation credential itself). The supplier needs no vLEI: its LEI identifies it, and the Commission's guidance for non-EU operators lists the LEI as an accepted identifier for operators from countries such as Taiwan [1]. Besides the LEI, it needs an EVM wallet to claim shipments and the signed credential its verification body issues. The demo also issues the supplier a Legal Entity vLEI, marked optional. For context: on 2026-09-23 the GLEIF website listed 8 qualified vLEI issuers (QVIs); GLEIF does not publish how many vLEIs have been issued.

**Q14. How is a batch ID bound to the physical goods?**
In this prototype it is not; this is a known limitation. The supplier chooses the batch ID. The contract makes each batch claimable once and caps all claims at the verified tonnage, so a new batch ID cannot create tonnes beyond the report, but it cannot prove the goods came from that installation. Roadmap: bind the batch to the customs declaration or to mill heat numbers.

**Q15. If we redeploy and rerun, will the importer's check still pass after the report expires?**
Yes, by design, for the importer the shipment was declared to. Validity is judged at the shipment's claim time when the importer verifies with its own EORI (check 5 binds the batch to it): the on-chain check then uses `isValidAt(reportKey, t)` with `t` set to the shipment's `claimedAt`, so a shipment claimed before `validUntil` still verifies when the importer declares the following year. Anyone else, including a verifier that gives no EORI, gets the report judged at the latest block, so an expired or superseded report fails for them. Revocation still applies immediately: a revoked report marks all its shipments invalid. When a verification body's institution record moves to a new current address, the old address is invalidated for new registrations immediately, but a registered report's validity is never recomputed against later authority changes, so older reports stay valid. Contract tests cover expiry, rotation and the claim-time check. A redeployment gives new contract addresses and a new EIP-712 domain, so credentials must be re-signed, or the old contracts kept read-only (SECURITY.md §5). This repository did that once: the earlier deployment is kept in `contracts/deployments/archive/`, and the demo credential was issued again for the current contracts.

**Q16. Registered operators can already share verified values with every declarant through the Registry. Against that baseline, how many extra tonnes do you save?**
Inside the Registry, CarbonLEI adds transparency, not tonnes: machine-checkable authority and a public claim record. There the verified value already replaces the default value, so we count no extra tonnes for CarbonLEI ([CLIMATE_IMPACT §4.4](CLIMATE_IMPACT.md#44-attribution-what-carbonlei-itself-adds)).
CarbonLEI's effect lies outside the Registry (downstream buyers, banks, product passports, the e-signed PDF copy in 2027) and across importers: the verification body's authority stays machine-checkable wherever the report travels, and the tonnage ledger adds a voluntary cap on the tonnes claimed against one verified value. Its size is unknown: we cannot estimate how many verified values will travel outside the Registry. We do not quantify the effect of the tonnage ledger; in the demo, it rejects a 400 t claim for a second importer when 300 t of the 500 t remain (a dry run against the live Sepolia contract that returns `ExceedsVerifiedTonnage`).

**Q17. How does lowering the CBAM burden count as a climate benefit? The mark-up is deliberately conservative.**
We do not count a lower CBAM bill as a climate benefit. The mark-up exists to push operators to provide verified data, and CarbonLEI works in the same direction: it makes verified values checkable and single-use. A low verified value can replace the default only for the tonnes it actually covers, so real emissions do not drop out of declarations. Accurate declarations then let lower-carbon production earn a price advantage. The causal chain, with the CBAM articles it rests on and its limits: [How it connects to climate outcomes](CLIMATE_IMPACT.md#how-it-connects-to-climate-outcomes).

**Q18. How many verification reports will exist in Taiwan in 2027 for importers to use?**
We do not know. In the Commission's state of play of 7 October 2026, 24 EU/EEA national accreditation bodies had agreed to offer CBAM accreditation, but only 7 were accepting applications from verifiers outside the EU (5 on 29 September); the Commission's list of accredited verifiers had not been published by 10 October 2026, and the first verification reports are expected in January 2027 [2]. Early volume will be small. That is why we built one corridor end to end (Taiwan fasteners into the EU) instead of claiming wide coverage.

**Q19. If you remove the AI (or rule) layer, what does the system lose?**
The seven verification checks do not depend on it and keep working.
What it loses is check 8: the auditable record that the verification report and the signed credential agree, and the regulatory checks (accreditation scope, first-year site visit, assurance level, materiality, dates). The input, output and rule-version hashes of that comparison sit in the credential core, which the `credSAID` covers; the lead auditor anchors that `credSAID` and the verification body signs it. Without this layer, those signatures no longer cover a machine-checkable comparison between report and credential.

**Q20. Why not just use a signed database?**
A signed database works when every party trusts whoever runs it. Here the writers are several verification bodies and suppliers, and the readers are importers in different member states. The part that needs a record none of them controls is the tonnage ledger shared across importers: in the demo, a 400 t claim for a second importer is rejected because only 300 t of the 500 t remain. What is not decentralised yet is the allowlist itself (see Q1 in the [README](../README.md#six-questions-reviewers-ask)). See [Where CarbonLEI sits, and why a ledger](../README.md#where-carbonlei-sits-and-why-a-ledger).
A second part needs it too: for reports used outside the CBAM Registry, the rule of one verification report per installation and reporting period is enforced across verification bodies. No single body's database sees the other bodies' reports; the contract rejects a second report for the same installation and period with `PeriodAlreadyCovered` unless it names the report it supersedes, and only the original issuing body may supersede its own report unless that body has been suspended or its accreditation has expired.

**Q22. Why not eIDAS signatures?**
They solve part of the problem, and CarbonLEI does not replace them. In the EU a qualified electronic signature has the legal effect of a handwritten one (Regulation (EU) No 910/2014, Art. 25(2) [3]). Its certificate names the signatory and may name their organisation, including its LEI (ETSI EN 319 412-2, NAT-4.2.4-6; EN 319 412-1, clause 5.1.4 [5]), and validation checks that the certificate was valid at the time of signing (Art. 32 [3]). A PAdES signature can also carry a signer role, either claimed by the signer or certified by an attribute authority (ETSI EN 319 122-1, clause 5.2.6.1 [5]).
What a standard qualified certificate does not carry is what the CBAM rules ask for: that the person who authenticates the verification report is "duly authorised to do so by the verifier" (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/2551, Annex II, point 2.15.2 [4]), and that the verifier was "duly accredited at the time" (Commission verification guidance, p.123). An electronic attestation of attributes could express this, since eIDAS lists "powers and mandates to represent" (Annex VI [3]), but we found none issued for CBAM verifiers, and the European Business Wallet proposal, which covers mandates, is still in the legislative process (2025/0358(COD) [7]). The guidance calls the 2027 copy an "electronically signed PDF" (p.68) and does not say whether the signature is qualified or advanced, or who applies it.
Neither eIDAS signatures nor attestations track the tonnes claimed across importers; that is what the ledger adds. The two approaches can be combined: GLEIF says it is "working toward the acceptance of the vLEI as an electronic attestation of attributes" (September 2024 [6]); the vLEI is not today an attestation under eIDAS, and CBAM does not recognise vLEIs (Q12).

**Q23. What if a report is revoked after the importer has declared?**
What CarbonLEI shows: the verification body revokes with `revokeReport`. The revocation is public (a `ReportRevoked` event with its block time, and `revokedAt` in the report record) and cannot be undone. From then on, every check of a proof for that report fails check 4 with `REPORT_INVALID/REVOKED` and the verdict is INVALID (CLI exit code 1), whenever the shipment was claimed: unlike expiry or supersession, a revocation is not judged at the claim time (Q15), and `shipmentStatus` returns the shipment's report as no longer valid. The exception is a revocation that looks like a stolen key's: if the address that sent it was rotated away within 24 hours after it, or was rotated in and away again within 30 days, the verdict is CONTESTED (exit code 2) and a person decides ([OPERATE, Incident procedures](OPERATE.md#incident-procedures), step 1).
What it does not show: the verdict's detail does not print when the report was revoked. The importer can read that from the `ReportRevoked` event and compare it with the block time of its own claim transaction, which stays on-chain unchanged; the verification record and importer's file it saved when it accepted the value (ADOPTION §2.1) are unsigned copies of the checks at that time. CarbonLEI runs no notification service, but the importer can schedule `carbonlei recheck` on its accepted records (Q29): it re-verifies them, reports `REPORT_REVOKED` as needing action, and can POST the change to the importer's own webhook; in ledger-only mode (a record without its proof) it also prints the revocation time from `revokedAt`. What the importer must then do under CBAM is set by the CBAM rules and its competent authority, not by CarbonLEI, and we do not describe a procedure here; a CBAM declaration can be reviewed after submission (Regulation (EU) 2023/956, Art 19, ADOPTION §1.1).

**Re-check after the window.** A VALID verdict obtained less than `contestedWindowHours` (default 24 hours) after the report's registration can still become CONTESTED: an auditor revocation or body suspension that the watcher syncs within that window is checked against the registration block (SECURITY T2, T18). An importer that relies on a value soon after registration re-checks it once the window has passed and keeps both results: `carbonlei recheck` reports `WINDOW_OPEN` while the window is open and `BECAME_FINAL` once it has closed with no event (Q29). The Maude model reaches this case ([SECURITY §9.3](SECURITY.md#93-protocol-model-maude)); it is an operating rule, not a new attack.

**Q24. How does CarbonLEI relate to EBSI, eIDAS 2 / EUDI and European Business Wallets, and the CBAM Registry?**
Facts first (each from a primary source, accessed 2026-10-09):
- **EBSI.** The European Blockchain Services Infrastructure is now "operated by Europeum-EDIC on behalf of participating EU Member States"; its site dates the network technical launch to 2026 and lists the business launch as upcoming [8]. The Commission's last EBSI newsflash, of 2 February 2026, said it "will cease providing services for EBSI, starting from February 2026" [9]. EBSI's issuer trust model is a chain of accreditations: a Root TAO accredits Trusted Accreditation Organisations (TAOs), which accredit Trusted Issuers (TIs); the accreditations are stored "within a smart contract as Verifiable Accreditations" in the EBSI Issuers Registry ("a generic decentralised registry that maintains information about trusted issuers"; earlier API versions call it the Trusted Issuers Registry), each participation "can be revoked separately", and a TI must have an entry in the EBSI DID registry and be invited into a trust chain [10]. EBSI states that "the accreditation of TIs is domain-specific and falls outside the scope of EBSI" [10]. We found no EBSI trust chain for CBAM verifiers, and no statement on whether organisations outside the EU can be onboarded.
- **eIDAS 2 and the EUDI Wallet.** Regulation (EU) 2024/1183 (11 April 2024) amends eIDAS to set up the European Digital Identity Framework: each Member State provides at least one European Digital Identity Wallet "within 24 months of the date of entry into force of the implementing acts" (Art. 5a(1)), and an electronic attestation of attributes "shall not be denied legal effect" for being electronic (Art. 45b) [11]. Attestations can carry "powers and mandates to represent" (recital 55; Annex VI, Q22).
- **European Business Wallets.** Still a proposal: COM(2025) 838 of 19 November 2025 [12]. The Council adopted its negotiating position on 9 June 2026; Parliament's ITRE committee voted on 10 September 2026, and the plenary confirmed the decision to enter interinstitutional negotiations on 7 October 2026 [7]. The proposal's wallets would "issue, request, obtain … share and present electronic attestations of attributes", including attestations "linked to other relevant attestations forming part of a chain" (Art. 5(1)(a), (g)), lists the LEI and the EORI among attributes (recital 25), covers "representation rights and mandates", and allows recognition of equivalent systems from third countries (Chapter IV) [12].
- **CBAM Registry.** The Commission's platform for CBAM declarants, operators of non-EU installations and accredited verifiers, who issue their verification reports through its O3CI portal [13]. It is not open to the public (Q12).

What CarbonLEI shares with them: an issuer accreditation chain. EBSI's Root TAO → TAO → TI has the same shape as CarbonLEI's GLEIF root → QVI → NAB accreditation credential → verification body → lead auditor, and both let a verifier follow the chain up to a root and see revocations.

What differs:
1. **Root and roles.** CarbonLEI roots legal-entity identity and personal roles in GLEIF's vLEI chain (ISO 17442-3), with KERI key event logs for the auditor, so a check asks whether *this person* was authorised *by this body* for this report. EBSI anchors issuers in DIDs on its own registry; the CBAM accreditation itself is, in EBSI's words, domain-specific and out of its scope.
2. **A tonnage ledger across importers.** None of the four, as publicly documented, keeps a count of the tonnes claimed against one verified value across importers that parties outside the Registry can check: EBSI, eIDAS 2 attestations and the Business Wallets carry identities and attributes, not quantities, and the CBAM Registry receives every declarant's declarations but is not public and publishes no such count. That per-shipment ledger, capped at the verified tonnage within this deployment, per credential, is what CarbonLEI adds (Q12, Q16).
3. **Who can check.** The CBAM Registry is closed; a wallet attestation is shown to the relying party it is presented to. On a public chain, CarbonLEI's claim record and allowlist can be read by anyone, including buyers, banks and product passports outside the Registry.
CarbonLEI does not replace any of them and is not integrated with any of them.

Where CarbonLEI could plug in (a proposal; we have contacted no one at EBSI, Europeum, the Commission or GLEIF):
- *EBSI:* the NAB accreditation credential could be issued as an EBSI Verifiable Accreditation by a NAB onboarded as a TAO, with check 7 reading the EBSI Issuers Registry beside the vLEI chain; this needs a CBAM trust chain in EBSI that does not exist today. Running the tonnage ledger on EBSI itself would be one more deployment option, to be weighed by Q25's criterion (who can read and verify without joining); we have not evaluated it.
- *eIDAS 2 / Business Wallets:* the lead auditor's mandate could also travel as an electronic attestation of attributes, and the supplier's credential could be presented from a business wallet that carries its LEI; GLEIF says it is working toward the vLEI's acceptance as an attestation of attributes (Q22 [6]).
- *CBAM Registry:* nothing is submitted to it; the importer's file and PACT export are meant to sit beside a declaration (ADOPTION §1.1). There is no Registry XML export because the Commission has published no data structure for the definitive-period declaration; its Registry module opens in 2027 ([REGISTRY_EXPORT](REGISTRY_EXPORT.md)). For the operator data a declarant keeps, `carbonlei export-communication` writes the `Summary_Communication` fields of the Commission's communication template v2.1.1 as a CSV from a verified proof, filling only what the proof holds; it is not validated by the Commission and is not a Registry submission.

**Q25. Why not Hyperledger Fabric or another permissioned chain?**
A permissioned chain is not ruled out: [ADOPTION §6.1](ADOPTION.md#61-technical) lists a permissioned EVM chain as the production option if tonnages must stay confidential, with its state root anchored on a public chain. The deciding criterion is who can read and verify without joining: on a public chain a bank or buyer outside the pilot can; on a permissioned one only admitted participants can. We stayed on the EVM because the contracts, the SDK and the verifier's chain reads are written for it; moving to the EVM chain chosen only changes the deployment file and the chain ID in the EIP-712 domain. A Fabric version would mean rewriting the contracts as chaincode and the verifier's chain reads; we have not done or measured that.

**Q26. Why no token?**
The ledger only needs to count. Each claim records a batch, a salted importer commitment and a quantity against one credential, and the contract rejects any claim beyond the verified tonnage; a claim is tied to a shipment and is not transferred. A token would add transfer and trading, which the CBAM flow we model does not need, and would make verified tonnes look like a tradable asset; CarbonLEI does not create or trade carbon credits. The contracts have no payable functions and no token transfers ([SECURITY §5](SECURITY.md#5-smart-contract-assurance)).

**Q27. Why a 24-hour CONTESTED window?**
The window covers the time between an off-chain event (an ECR revocation, an accreditation withdrawal, a stolen key found) and the watcher's on-chain sync: a report registered within 24 hours before that sync is flagged CONTESTED for a person to review, not marked invalid ([ARCHITECTURE §8](ARCHITECTURE.md#8-time-ordering)). 24 hours is a default (`contestedWindowHours`), not a value derived from a measurement or a rule; the stated requirement is that it "must stay well above the watcher's sync latency", which was 16 seconds in one Sepolia run (assumption A-Watch, [SECURITY §9](SECURITY.md#9-security-properties)). What it trades off: a larger window catches a later response but flags more routine rotations as CONTESTED (about 0.27 % of a body's yearly reports per rotation or suspension without the routine procedure); and a thief can wait, so a stolen key found more than 24 hours after a revocation it sent leaves that revocation INVALID ([OPERATE, Incident procedures](OPERATE.md#incident-procedures) and [Routine key rotation](OPERATE.md#routine-key-rotation)).

**Q28. An importer has dozens of suppliers. Does it have to verify proofs one by one and work out its declaration by hand?**

No. `carbonlei verify-batch` verifies a folder or manifest of proofs at one pinned block, and `carbonlei portfolio` plans the year from the verdicts: a line uses the supplier's verified value only when its proof is VALID and check 5 bound it to the importer; otherwise the default with the year's mark-up, with the reason. The plan gives totals by CN code, origin, installation, supplier and quarter, the 50 t de minimis status, the quarterly holding that applies from 2027, the certificates and their gross €, and a CSV worksheet (not a CBAM Registry format; why there is none yet, and where each field goes in the Commission's communication template: [REGISTRY_EXPORT](REGISTRY_EXPORT.md)). The hosted page's Importer workbench tab shows the same plan for one live and five illustrative lines. Rules and sources: [OPERATE › Importer workbench](OPERATE.md#importer-workbench-planning-a-years-declaration).

**Q29. Most of a screw's emissions come from the wire rod. Does CarbonLEI check the wire rod's value too?**
Yes, when the screw credential commits to it (check 9, multi-tier). Under the CBAM methodology a complex good's value adds its precursors' embedded emissions: direct emissions of the screw process plus, per precursor, the tonnes consumed per tonne of screws (scrap included) times the precursor's value; actual precursor data need the producer's valid verification report, otherwise the precursor default with mark-up applies (Commission Guidance No. 3, section 4.2.2.3; Guidance No. 5d, p. 14 and Example 3). The screw maker's verification body signs a precursor statement into the screw credential; the wire-rod mill's report is a credential of its own, from its own verification body and lead auditor, in the same registry; and the mill claims the delivered wire rod for the screw maker's credential (its LEI and the credential's SAID) against that report, so the same verified wire rod cannot back two screw makers beyond its verified tonnes (`ExceedsVerifiedTonnage`), nor two credentials of one maker. Check 9 verifies the mill's proof with the same checks, recomputes the screws' value within 0.01 tCO2e/t, and flags a precursor default. Limits: shown on a local anvil chain with synthetic vLEI evidence, not on Sepolia; the mass per tonne is the screw body's signed statement, not measured; precursor defaults are not read from the Default Values Act; the mill's identity is disclosed to the buyer ([SECURITY §4.3](SECURITY.md#43-check-9-precursors)).

**Q30. Enterprise CBAM tools send alerts and offer an API. Does CarbonLEI tell the importer when an accepted value goes bad, and can its systems call it?**

Yes, as prototypes the importer runs itself. `carbonlei recheck <records>` re-verifies every value the importer accepted (the Buyer tab's verification record, which now carries the proof, or `verify-batch` results) at one pinned block and compares it with the accepted verdict. Each line gets a change and a severity: **action** for a revoked or superseded report, a CONTESTED verdict (auditor revoked or body suspended within 24 h after registration, registering address rotated, a revocation from a possibly stolen key), a claim no longer on the ledger or another failing check; **info** when the CONTESTED window has closed with no event (`BECAME_FINAL`); **error** when the re-check could not run, which never counts as "unchanged". It exits 1 when any line needs action, so a daily scheduled job ([example workflow](examples/importer-recheck.yml)) fails and alerts; `--webhook` POSTs the changed lines without the EORI; `portfolio --recheck` plans action lines on the default. `carbonlei serve` exposes verify, verify-batch, portfolio and recheck as a REST API ([OpenAPI](schemas/openapi.json)) with size, time and concurrency limits and no personal data in its logs. It has no authentication: it binds 127.0.0.1 and must sit behind the importer's own gateway ([OPERATE](OPERATE.md#rest-api-carbonlei-serve-deployment-and-security), [SECURITY T24–T27](SECURITY.md#14-http-api-carbonlei-serve)). An [ERP integration example](../examples/erp-integration/) maps a generic ERP export (a CSV of purchase-order lines with CN code, origin, net mass, import date and proof file) to the API, checks the HMAC binding of the results, and writes a CSV back for the ERP with the verdict, verified or default value, a reason code and a certificates estimate per line; it is not a CBAM Registry filing and not a SAP or Oracle connector. What it does not do: CarbonLEI operates no hosted alerting service or API, there is no push feed from the chain (the re-check polls), and nothing is sent to the CBAM Registry.

**Q31. Taiwan has a carbon fee. Can the screws' CBAM bill be reduced by it, and does CarbonLEI check that?**
On draft rules, and the honest number is small. CBAM Art 9 allows a deduction for the carbon price effectively paid in the country of origin, but its method exists only as a Commission draft implementing act (Ares(2026)4841230, 13 May 2026), not adopted, with no country recognised ([status](https://cbamguide.com/compliance/carbon-price-deduction/), accessed 2026-10-16). Most screw makers emit below Taiwan's 25,000 t threshold and pay no fee; the steel mill does, and under the draft a certified price paid on a precursor counts. Check 10 verifies such a statement with the same trust model: the mill signs it, an independent person accredited for the draft's activity group LIV certifies it (its lead auditor anchors it in their KEL, its body registers it in the same registry), the mill claims the wire rod it delivered to the screw maker's credential against it so the price cannot be used twice (a statement on the good itself is claimed for one shipment), the mass per tonne comes only from a precursor statement the screw body signed, and the verifier re-derives the price with its own pinned exchange rate. A high-leakage mill pays NT$300 on 20 % of its emissions ([Regulations Governing the Collection of Carbon Fees, Arts 5–6](https://oaout.moenv.gov.tw/laW/LawContent.aspx?id=GL007914)): about €1.70 per tonne emitted, against €82.32 per certificate; for the demo's 200 t of screws, 6.709 of 360 certificates (1.9 %). Every output says "Draft implementing act, not adopted — illustrative"; shown on a local anvil chain with a fictional certifier ([SECURITY §4.4](SECURITY.md#44-check-10-carbon-price-paid-draft-art-9-rules), [CLIMATE_IMPACT §5.1](CLIMATE_IMPACT.md#51-carbon-price-interplay-taiwans-fee-and-cbam-draft-art-9)).

**Q32. Every EU customer asks my company for the same data. Do I have to answer each one separately?**

No. Keep one allocation book per verified report: per batch, the buyer (its EORI and a per-batch salt, which stay with you), the tonnes, whether the claim is sent, and which fields that buyer may see. `carbonlei allocate <book.json>` checks the book against the registry at one block: tonnes per buyer, what is left of the verified tonnes, claims missing or differing from the book, planned batches the contract would refuse because they exceed what is left, and on-chain claims your book does not list. `carbonlei present-batch <book.json> --out <dir>` then writes one proof per buyer and batch in a single run, each with only that buyer's fields. A field list that hides one check 8 needs is refused (`FIELDS_INCOMPLETE` in `allocate`, the batch skipped by `present-batch` unless `--allow-incomplete`), because the buyer's verifier would return INCOMPLETE, never VALID; the fewest fields that still verify are 18. Each proof verifies only for its own buyer's EORI (check 5), so a buyer cannot reuse another buyer's proof, and the shared ledger stops the same verified tonnes going to two buyers on the same deployment. The Commission's O3CI module also lets operators share data once with all declarants, inside the CBAM Registry ([CBAM Registry page](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism/cbam-registry_en), accessed 2026-10-16); CarbonLEI proofs also work outside it, for example for a bank or a downstream buyer, and carry the shared cap. Limits: the book is a file on your computer, not a hosted portal; the CLI sends no transaction (you send each claim from your wallet); and the salts in the book are secrets ([OPERATE](OPERATE.md#supplier-allocations-one-report-many-buyers), [SECURITY T32–T34](SECURITY.md#supplier-allocation-book)). The hosted Supplier tab's Allocations section shows it on the real Sepolia claim and two fictional planned buyers.

**Q33. Someone sent us a "verification statement" for our supplier's 2026 data. How do we know it is a real CBAM verification, from a verifier that was allowed to issue it?**

Check 8 now checks the report itself, not only that it matches the credential. Accredited verifiers can issue the first CBAM verification reports from January 2027 (Commission timeline) and verification "cannot be concluded before January of the subsequent year" (Commission guidance, 24 August 2026, §4.1), so a report for 2026 dated in 2026 fails; so does a report whose accreditation had expired on its date (§7.3: the verifier must be "duly accredited … at the time of issuing the verification statement"), and a first-year report without a physical site visit, unless the verifier invoked Art 4 of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2546 (serious, extraordinary and unforeseeable circumstances), which a person then reviews. These fail check 8 (INVALID) only when the auditor signed the report (the hashes in the credential match); a statement without that signature gets the same findings as warnings, and for a credential that carries the hashes, an extract that is missing, changed or partly hidden makes the result INCOMPLETE, never VALID. With the trust-registry operator's accreditation record (`carbonlei verify --accreditation-registry <file>`; always on the hosted page, Trust chain › Verifier legitimacy), check 8 also shows who accredited the body: the national accreditation body and its country, the accreditation number, EN ISO/IEC 17029, the activity group and CN scope, and the validity on the report date. The Commission had not published its list of accredited CBAM verifiers when we checked (16 October 2026); five bodies were documented as accredited on 13 October 2026, all in the EU. What check 8 cannot see: whether the report was issued in the CBAM Registry, which is not public, and whether a stated site visit took place. Details: [SECURITY §4.5](SECURITY.md#45-check-8-sub-rules-on-the-verification-report), threats T35–T38. Sources: 14 below.
